## Question

How can a student book one tutoring session without having to guess the price or the available times first?

## Business and one workflow

- Business (proposed, team to confirm): a small tutoring service where students book one-on-one sessions with peer tutors.
- Workflow: a student picks a tutor, picks an open time slot, and confirms one session.
- Customer task to solve: confirm a single booking for a chosen tutor and time.

### R1 Wyzant

- Observation: the homepage presents a large network of independent tutors and lists three steps (choose a tutor, share goals, book a lesson) without explaining them. It offers subject and city shortcuts, a Find a Tutor link and a first-hour Good Fit Guarantee. It shows no prices, calendars or availability on the homepage.
- Interpretation (tentative): trust is emphasized, but a student must go further to learn the cost and the times.

### R2 Varsity Tutors

- Observation: the homepage asks first for a learner grade level and offers a chat, a short form and a matching quiz. It says a team member can discuss availability and pricing, and shows no booking calendar, time slots or prices.
- Interpretation (tentative): it guides students with questions, and defers times and price to a conversation.

### R3 Calendly (adjacent)

- Observation: hosts set availability, share a booking link, and the invitee picks a slot. Invitees can reschedule on their own, reminders are sent, and a deposit can be collected at booking. The homepage shows no pricing tiers.
- Interpretation (tentative): a useful pattern for the booking step only (slot choice and self-rescheduling). It is not a tutoring service, so its audience differs.

## Two positions

- **Position A, browse first:** show tutors, open slots and the session price together, so a student picks a slot directly. Strength: removes guessing. Risk: needs accurate availability data.
- **Position B, match first:** ask a few questions (subject, course level), then recommend a tutor and open slots. Strength: helps students who do not know whom to choose. Risk: adds steps before the student sees a price.
- Chosen (proposed, team to confirm): Position A, because both tutoring references defer times and price while the booking reference shows slot choice. B stays a possible later feature.

## Audience choice (proposed)

Persona 1. Her task, booking a single session, is small and observable. Persona 2 is deferred. This research does not show that students want this service, or how many would use it.

## Source to decision to check

- Observation: R1 and R2 do not show price or open slots on their homepages, and R3 shows slot choice and self-rescheduling.
- Proposed feature: a tutor page that lists open slots and the session price before any signup.
- Check (planned, not run): a reviewer starting at the tutor list finds a price and an open slot within 30 seconds.
- Still needs testing: whether students actually want price and times first. There is no customer evidence yet.

## Limits
